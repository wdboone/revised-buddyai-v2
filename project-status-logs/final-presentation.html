The problem

Why someone would use Buddy

Most people working through career changes, money stress, or family strain don't have anyone who knows their whole situation. Professional coaching is expensive and episodic. A generic chatbot starts from zero every time: it forgets you, drifts into medical or legal advice, and has no plan for a crisis.

It remembers you

Each reply distills durable facts into a persistent user model the user can view and edit.

It stays aligned

Eight life domains, auto-routed per conversation. Role and safety boundaries are written into the prompt and verified with a red-team suite, crisis handling included.

It's grounded

Replies draw on live web search with cited sources, and accounts sync across devices with per-user data isolation.

Added as we learned

  • Guardrail red-team spike. Guardrails were "done" in Week 2 but never measured. The spike found three real gaps.
  • Prompt hardening. The fix for those three gaps.
  • Server-side API key. Known limitation, promoted once real accounts existed.
  • File upload → user model. Lets users teach Buddy faster than chat alone.
  • Signup-redirect fix. Found by the first real signup.

Postponed

  • Output safety classifier. Designed, then deferred once prompt hardening alone reached 0% measured attack success.
  • Tiered memory retrieval. Matters once a user's history outgrows the context window.
  • Per-user rate limiting. Needed at public scale.

What we learned

Willie Boone

Lead Developer
  • Real infrastructure has invisible defaults. The Week 3 permissions bug taught me to read errors carefully and trace problems to their root cause, not guess at fixes.
  • Guardrails have to be measured, not assumed. They looked "done" in Week 2, but the red-team spike found real gaps that ordinary conversation testing never would have surfaced.
  • Directing AI well is a skill of its own. Reviewing, testing, and refining AI-generated code turned out to be just as demanding as writing it by hand — and just as important to get right.
