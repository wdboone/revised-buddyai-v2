# Theme Bank

The life domains for the self-interview. Each is one cell of a coverage grid: get every domain to adequate
coverage — **two or three concrete dated scenes, a stated value, and something on the life arc** — rather than
exhausting any one of them. Run roughly accessible → tender. Combine two thin, adjacent domains into one session
when the subject has little in either.

Each domain names the three levels to hit:
- **Traits** — inferred from recurring behavior; watch, don't ask.
- **Concerns** — goals, values, coping; probe directly.
- **Story** — the arc; get the scene.

Close every session by asking what didn't get said. (Sample of the full grid below.)

---

## 1. Branching points
**Framing.** The forks where life could have gone another way — and did, or didn't.
**Priming.** Three or four moments where your life changed direction: a move, a choice, a chance meeting, a door
that closed. Which forks did you choose, and which were chosen for you?
**Opening.** "Tell me about a time your life forked — where, looking back, things could have gone a completely
different way."
**Stop when.** You have the fork as a scene, the value under the choice, and how they frame it now.

## 2. Family and where you come from
**Framing.** The people you were handed, and what they gave and cost you.
**Priming.** Who raised you, and who else shaped you early? A family story that gets retold — and one that never
gets told?
**Opening.** "Paint me your family as you knew it growing up — the cast of characters."
**Stop when.** You have a scene per central figure, one thing kept and one rejected, and the untold story named.

## 3. Work and vocation
**Framing.** What you do, why you do it, and what it costs.
**Priming.** The jobs that formed you, the one that broke you, the work you'd do for free.
**Opening.** "Walk me through how you ended up doing what you do now."
**Stop when.** You have a defining work scene, the value it serves, and where the arc is heading.

## 4. Health and the body
**Framing.** The physical and mental facts you live around.
**Priming.** A time your body or mind forced a change in plans. What you've learned to manage.
**Opening.** "Tell me about a stretch where your health set the terms."
**Stop when.** You have a concrete episode, how they cope, and what it changed.

*(Remaining domains — home and place, friendship, love and partnership, money, faith and meaning, failure and
repair, and what's next — follow the same shape.)*
