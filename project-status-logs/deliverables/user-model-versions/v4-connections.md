# Connections — the cross-cutting layer

Patterns that link facts across the three layers into conclusions no single fact states. Each one does, at
storage time, the connecting a coach would otherwise have to do on the fly. Every link rests on facts already in
the model.

*Example artifact. Fictional persona.*

- **Her whole working setup is an accommodation for her anxiety, not just a lifestyle preference.** Working from
  home as a freelancer suits the anxiety that makes exposure to strangers hard, and setting her own schedule suits
  someone who withdraws under stress. What looks like a career choice is partly a coping strategy.
  Links: traits/works-from-home ↔ traits/anxiety ↔ traits/withdraws-under-stress. Confidence: high.

- **The same trait that protects her — investing in a few deep ties — is what made the move so costly.** Her
  few-close-friends strategy works until the few people are suddenly 2,000 miles away, and then the anxiety and the
  withdrawal pattern keep her from rebuilding. One disposition explains both her old stability and her current
  isolation. Links: traits/few-deep-friendships ↔ concerns/rebuilding-social-life ↔ traits/withdraws-under-stress.
  Confidence: high.

- **The leap to freelance and the pull back toward her father are the same question asked twice.** Both her proudest
  decision (choosing autonomy over security) and the reckoning after his health scare turn on how she wants to spend
  a finite life. The father who taught her to draw is tied to the craft she left the agency to protect.
  Links: story/the-leap ↔ story/fathers-health-scare ↔ traits/craft-over-polish. Confidence: med.

- **Withdrawal is the thread connecting her isolation, her father-distance, and her slow social rebuild.** The
  short-run-protective, long-run-isolating pattern shows up in three separate places in the model. Naming it once
  explains all three. Links: traits/withdraws-under-stress ↔ concerns/rebuilding-social-life ↔ concerns/fathers-health.
  Confidence: high.
