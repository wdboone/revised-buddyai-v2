# v1 — Unstructured baseline (Dana Whitfield)

*Example artifact. Fictional persona.*

Dana is a freelance graphic designer in her late thirties who works from home. She left a stable job at a
design agency about three years ago after a long stretch of burnout, and went out on her own to control her own
schedule and take only work she believes in. The freedom matters to her more than the income stability she gave
up, though the irregular money is a steady low-grade worry. She lives with her partner Miguel and a dog named
Biscuit; they have no kids. Two years ago they moved from Chicago to Portland for Miguel's job, and the move,
while good for them as a couple, cut Dana off from the small circle of close friends she'd spent a decade
building.

She is an introvert who has always kept a few deep friendships rather than a wide network, and the move exposed
how much that strategy costs when the few people are suddenly far away. She's been slow and reluctant about
rebuilding a social life in a new city, partly temperament and partly the anxiety she's managed since her
twenties, which flares when she has to put herself in front of strangers. Working from home suits the anxiety
but deepens the isolation, and she knows it.

Her father still lives in Chicago and had a health scare earlier this year — a cardiac event that turned out to
be survivable but frightening. It pulled her back home for two weeks and reopened an old, complicated closeness
with him; he was the parent who taught her to draw, and also the one whose approval she spent years chasing. She
came back from that trip shaken and thinking harder about how she spends her time.

Underneath the specifics, Dana values autonomy, craft over polish, and depth over breadth — in her work, her
friendships, and how she presents herself. She's quietly proud of having made the leap to freelance, and quietly
afraid it won't hold. Her recurring pattern is to retreat when things get hard, which protects her in the short
run and isolates her over time.
